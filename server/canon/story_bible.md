## Standing facts (always safe)
- The serial is *Aakhri Awaaz*, a true-crime podcast, 8 episodes, set in Bhopal.
- MEERA JOSHI, 28, hosts it from a converted storeroom in her family home. Sharp, dry-humoured, allergic to sentimentality. Speaks Hinglish.
- Her grandfather, the late DINANATH JOSHI, was a respected schoolteacher who raised her. His framed photo hangs above her mic.
- IQBAL CHACHA, 61, retired Bhopal Police inspector, her late father's closest friend. Gravel voice, endless chai, a policeman's memory and a poet's regrets.
- THE VOICE is a serial extortionist known only by his voice. Calm, courteous, never shouts. No photographs of him exist.

<!-- EPISODE: 1 -->
## Ep 1 — "Pehli Call"
Meera covers a spate of "voice extortions": small businessmen get calls from a man who knows impossible details about their lives. No demands at first — just proof that he knows. Then ruin. She names him on air, half-joking: *The Voice*. That night her podcast voicemail plays her own intro back to her, slowed down, followed by: "Sundar awaaz hai. Dada ji par gayi ho."

<!-- EPISODE: 2 -->
## Ep 2 — "Jo Sunta Hai"
Meera interviews the victims. Every ruined man shares something she cannot yet see. Iqbal warns her off but pulls old case files anyway. The Voice calls a victim while Meera is sitting in his shop, and describes the colour of her dupatta.

<!-- EPISODE: 3 -->
## Ep 3 — "1994"
Every victim connects to one old case: *State vs. Haribhai Saluja*, 1994 — the investigating officer, the court clerk, the man who bought the Saluja godown at auction. Meera pulls the file and finds a name on the witness list: **D. Joshi, schoolteacher.** She tells no one. Not Iqbal. Not the podcast.

<!-- EPISODE: 4 -->
## Ep 4 — "Neem"
Meera visits the ruined Saluja house in Old Bhopal. The neem tree still stands. An old neighbour remembers the family: the mother who sang at the gurudwara, and the younger boy who could mimic any voice — "poora mohalla hansta tha." That night The Voice calls Meera directly for the first time, warmly, like an uncle: "Recording sambhal ke rakhna, Meera. Awaaz hi toh bachti hai aakhir mein."

<!-- EPISODE: 5 -->
## Ep 5 — "Sehore"
Meera traces SHAKUNTALA SALUJA to an institution in Sehore. The old woman has not spoken in years, but her lips move when Meera plays gurudwara shabads. A nurse mentions one regular visitor — "her son" — who always calls ahead, never faces a camera, pays cash. Meera's phone rings: "Maa se milne aayi ho? ...Achha laga." She realises he is not watching her. He is *listening*.

<!-- EPISODE: 6 -->
## Ep 6 — "Gawah"
The retired officer from the 1994 case, dying, tells Meera the truth: the testimony was bought, the real racketeer was **ZORAWAR SETHI**, and Dinanath Joshi was pressured — but signed knowing. Her grandfather condemned an innocent man. She records the confession, deletes it, and at 3 AM un-deletes it.

## The 1994 wound (known to the listener from Ep 6 onward)
Haribhai Saluja ran a small transport business. Dinanath Joshi testified that he saw Haribhai collecting extortion money outside the Hamidia Road godown on 14 August 1994. He had seen *a* man, not Haribhai. Haribhai got eleven years and died in Central Jail in 2003. Shakuntala stopped singing, then stopped speaking, and was left in Sehore. The elder son Randheer ran away at 16 and is presumed dead near Itarsi.

<!-- EPISODE: 7 -->
## Ep 7 — "Kabootar"
Meera decides to tell the story straight on the podcast, her grandfather's guilt included. She tells Iqbal everything; he admits he was a young constable in '94, that he carried files for that case, that everyone knew and no one spoke. Before she can record, the studio goes dark. A voice from the doorway — the first time it is not on a phone: "Episode achha likha hai. Par ending main sunaunga." Struggle. Static. The feed cuts.

The episode ends on found audio — Meera whispering into a phone he does not know she has: "Agar koi sun raha hai... main zinda hoon. Aur main record kar rahi hoon."

**The listener's phone is that hidden line.**

<!-- EPISODE: 8 -->
## Ep 8 — "Sutradhar"
Meera wakes in a storeroom and recognises the smell of neem: he has brought her to the Saluja house. He has taken her SIM, so calls meant for her now reach the listener. A power cut; she finds an old torch; three polite knocks; The Voice speaks through the door and admits he left the torch there. Then he calls the listener directly. Later he returns, and Meera has ten seconds of absolute silence to survive.

<!-- EPISODE: 9 -->
## Ep 9 and beyond — NOT KNOWN TO ANYONE YET
The Voice is **Chhotu Saluja**, Haribhai and Shakuntala's younger son, the boy who could mimic any voice. He intends to make Meera record the true story of 1994 in her own voice, as he has written it. Meera will go further than his script and name Zorawar Sethi, who is still alive and still respectable. Shakuntala will speak her younger son's name for the first time in twenty years.

**No character may reference any of this. The Voice never states his identity or full motive in Episode 8 — he only slips, once, about Dinanath.**
