The problem

Every story ever made is one-directional.

A book, an audiobook, a film, a podcast — the author decides the single path through it, and that is the only path you get. Read it twice and you get the same story, in the same order, told the same way. You can only explore it the way the author intended. And you cannot ask it anything: wonder why a character looked at the door, and the book does not answer. Nobody in there knows you are present.

Even the fixes didn't fix it

Choose-your-path books, Bandersnatch — a handful of pre-written paths with a menu on top. Every ending was already filmed. You are still picking from what the author decided; you are just picking.

Why it was always like this

A story had to be finished before it could be delivered. Every copy identical. That was a manufacturing constraint, not a creative one — and it is not true anymore.

The idea

So we stopped asking authors to write the path.

We ask them to write the world. An author gives us people with secrets, the conditions under which they give them up, and a clock that keeps running. Not a script. Then you explore it by talking — and where you end up depends on what you actually said, not on which button you pressed.

What we built

A story is a folder. The framework does the rest.

Story Framework plays 5–10 minute interactive stories in a mobile browser: one fixed scene, AI characters you can talk to, a clock that runs whether you act or not. Nothing story-specific lives in the framework's own code — every story ships as a JSON bundle plus assets, and the framework just plays it.

Tap. Type. Talk. Change your mind mid-scene.

The characters

It's not a script. It's a person with something to hide.

Every character is an authored persona — a personality, a voice, secrets they only give up under conditions the author set, and hard limits they never cross. Ask something the author never anticipated and you still get an answer: in character, from someone who knows exactly what they are protecting.

The AI

Nobody hard-coded the right answer.

When there is a challenge, you do not pick the correct option — you talk your way through it however you like. Then an LLM judge reads the entire conversation against a rubric the player never sees, and decides on its own whether you actually solved it.

Two players, same character

Completely different arguments, both of them valid. And one can fail while saying something that sounds perfectly reasonable — because nothing pre-decided what counted.

Why the rubric stays hidden

Show it and it becomes a password. Hide it from the player and from the character, and a free-text challenge becomes genuinely winnable — or losable.

The clock

A whole story day, every few real minutes.

An in-story clock turns real minutes into a full day; dawn, day, dusk and night crossfade the scene as time passes. It runs whether you say anything or not — hesitating is a decision. The world is happening; you are in it, not driving it. Two things do pause it: a reply in flight, and an open journal. Network lag never costs you story time, and thinking is free.

What you carry

You can reach where the author never pointed.

The journal holds your objective, the story so far, the clues you've found and who is reachable right now. Stories with kinship get a family tree: generations top to bottom, locked ones telling you exactly which clue opens them. Some of the people you can talk to are dead. Find the right clue and you get to ask your grandmother about the night she died — the author never wrote that scene. They made her reachable.

Who is listening

Every character reasons over your state and only your state. They cannot reference story you have not reached — enforced on the server, not asked for in a prompt.

The format

Stories are data. Not code.

A bundle is story.json (public), secrets.json (server-only — never shipped to a browser), and an assets/ folder. Both files are checked against a schema the moment the server boots; a broken bundle fails loud, before a single player ever sees it — never mid-game.

{
  "meta": {
    "title": "The Cellar",
    "tagline": "You have 3 days. Only you can do this.",
    "genre": "thriller",
    "modes": ["mcq", "text", "voice"]
  },
  "clock": {
    "realMinutesPerStoryDay": 5,
    "totalStoryDays": 3,
    "phases": ["dawn", "day", "dusk", "night"]
  }
}

— an unedited excerpt of stories/kidnapping-escape/story.json
