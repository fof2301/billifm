# format_fit.md — which stories fit the Event Track, and which fight it

Written for two audiences: P4 building the deck, and the Pocket FM content lead who
will ask "how much of our catalog can actually use this?"

The honest answer is **not all of it, and that is the point.** A format that suits
every episode suits none — scarcity is what makes it land (Design.md §1).

---

## 1. The one hard gate: a diegetic reason for the phone

Everything else here is negotiable. This is not.

**The listener must be a character in the fiction who plausibly has a line open.**

In *Aakhri Awaaz* the chain is airtight: Meera is abducted, keeps a hidden phone,
and it has exactly one number saved — yours. So when the villain takes her SIM, his
call *must* come to you. You are not "the audience". You are the last person on her
line.

Get that wrong and every effect degrades into a gimmick. The torch is thrilling
because *she found a torch*. If it turns on because the app thought a scary moment
deserved a flourish, the listener notices the app — and the moment the listener
notices the app, the fiction is over.

**Test:** can you finish the sentence *"the listener is the person who ______"*
without hedging? If not, the episode does not qualify. No amount of good writing
rescues it.

Four listener roles that work, and the question each answers:

| Role | The listener is… | Why they can't just hang up |
|---|---|---|
| **The Confidant** | the one person a trapped character trusts | leaving means abandoning them |
| **The Witness** | someone who heard what they shouldn't | now the threat knows you exist |
| **The Lifeline** | the only help reachable | you are load-bearing to their survival |
| **The Accomplice** | being drawn into complicity | you are already implicated |

*Aakhri Awaaz* uses Confidant, then converts you to Witness at the villain's call —
which is exactly why the call is the strongest beat in the episode.

## 2. The four secondary conditions

Each unlocks specific effects. An episode meeting the gate plus two of these is
worth doing; the gate plus all four is a showcase.

**a) Darkness must be motivated.** `screen_blackout` and `flashlight` need an
in-story cause: a power cut, a tunnel, a blown fuse, load-shedding, a cave, a
blackout curtain, eyes closing under anaesthetic. Night-set stories get this free.
A daylight office scene will never earn the torch.

**b) Whispering must be necessary.** `volume_duck` works because the listener leans
in — and leaning in only happens if the character has a real reason to drop their
voice. Someone nearby who must not hear. This one condition powers the format's
best effect and it is cheap to write for.

**c) The listener's real room must matter.** `mic_listen` needs a threat that could
plausibly hear *the line*, not just the character. This is the rarest condition and
the most valuable, because it is the only one no competitor can copy. It requires a
listening antagonist.

**d) Confinement.** Two to four sensory beats need a tight space and a short clock.
A story that moves through six locations dilutes every beat. One room, one night,
one problem.

## 3. Genres that fit

**Strong — the format was effectively designed for these**

- **Captivity / kidnap thriller.** The template. Confinement, darkness, whispering, a captor who calls.
- **Home invasion / siege.** Someone is inside the house. Whisper, dark, hide, do not make a sound. Every condition met without contrivance.
- **Supernatural horror — vigil and possession.** "Do not answer if it calls you by your name." Darkness is free, silence is doctrine, and the listener's own dark room becomes the set.
- **Trapped-survival.** Rubble after a quake, a flooded basement, a stalled lift, a collapsed mine. A phone is the only link out — the most natural diegetic anchor there is.
- **Stalker / extortion.** A caller with motive and patience. Asymmetric information. Works even in daylight, though you trade the torch.

**Workable — needs one deliberate structural choice**

- **Crime informant / witness protection.** Strong caller, strong asymmetry; you must manufacture the confinement (a safe house, a wire, a car boot).
- **War and conflict.** A soldier on a radio to a handler. Excellent whisper and darkness logic; the effect vocabulary must read as radio, not phone.
- **Medical emergency.** You are talking someone through a crisis. Powerful Lifeline role, but the tone is heroic rather than dreadful — expect the mic beat to feel wrong.
- **Heist.** You are the lookout. Great confinement and whispering; the listener has *tasks*, which cuts against reversed agency. Handle carefully.
- **Psychological / unreliable narrator.** The phone can lie to the listener. Rich, but hard to keep diegetic — the effects risk becoming metaphor, and metaphor is not a cause.

**Fights the format — do not force these**

- **Mythology and epics.** Sprawling geography, no telephony, ensemble cast. Everything the format needs is absent. A framing device (a devotee hearing a voice) is possible but it is a different show.
- **Period drama before the telephone.** No diegetic phone, so no anchor. Only a recording or seance conceit saves it, and that is a gimmick wearing the format's clothes.
- **Romance.** No motivated darkness, no reason to whisper, nothing listening. You would be adding effects because you can. (One exception: a long-distance call at 3AM where one person must stay quiet — that is one good scene, not an episode.)
- **Comedy.** Effects puncture jokes. Timing belongs to the comedian, not the track.
- **Courtroom and procedural.** Daylight, public, talkative, multi-location. Structurally opposed.
- **Ensemble / multi-POV.** The format lives on one intimate line. Split the POV and you split the intimacy.

## 4. The counter-intuitive rule: helplessness is the fuel

**The more agency the protagonist has, the worse the format works.**

An action hero does not need you. A captive does. Every effect in *Aakhri Awaaz*
comes from something being *done to* Meera — the power cut, the knocks, the call,
the man listening at the door. She is not choosing; she is enduring, and the phone
is the only thing she still controls.

Practical consequence: write the episode where your protagonist has the least
power. That is where the phone can act on the listener, because the phone is acting
on the character too.

## 5. Where it belongs in a serial

The format has a natural position in a season arc: **the episode of maximum
constraint**, which in most serials is the penultimate one.

That is not a coincidence in our showcase. Episode 8 of 8 is where Meera has lost
everything except the line — so the listener's phone becomes the last remaining
channel *exactly* when the story most needs one. Earlier episodes have no reason to
possess your phone; the finale needs its hands free for resolution.

**Guidance for retrofitting a catalog:** look for the episode where the protagonist
is most trapped and least able to act. Usually one or two per season. Not the
premiere (nobody has earned trust yet) and rarely the finale.

## 6. Catalog economics — the honest number

Assume a 40-episode serial. Realistically **1–3 episodes qualify.** That is not a
limitation to hide; it is the product argument:

- An Event Track is one JSON file. Marginal content cost is near zero, so the
  economics work even at 1-in-20 adoption.
- Rarity is what makes the "IMMERSIVE ⚡" badge mean something. If every episode
  were immersive, the badge would be furniture and the effects would be wallpaper.
- 1–3 unforgettable episodes per serial is a **retention** instrument, not a
  volume play. The pitch is not "we upgrade your catalog", it is "we give every
  serial the episode people tell their friends about."

Across a catalog of a thousand serials, 1–3 per serial is still one to three
thousand immersive episodes — and every one of them is a JSON file an agent can
write (M7).

## 7. The qualification checklist

Run any candidate episode through this. **The first question is a gate; failing it
disqualifies regardless of the rest.**

1. **Can the listener be named as a character with an open line?** *(gate)*
2. Is there a motivated reason for darkness?
3. Does someone have to whisper, and why?
4. Is there something in the fiction that could hear the listener's own room?
5. Is it one place, one night, one problem?
6. Is the protagonist more acted-upon than acting?
7. Is there a caller with a motive to reach the listener directly?

**1 + any two → worth an Event Track. 1 + five or more → make it the showcase.**

---

## 8. What we will not claim on stage

Consistent with scope.md §3.5:

- We will not claim every episode can be immersive. Asked directly, the answer is
  "one to three per season, and that scarcity is deliberate."
- We will not claim the format suits every genre. Romance and comedy are poor fits
  and saying so makes the thriller case credible.
- We will not claim the AI can decide *whether* an episode qualifies. It writes the
  direction for episodes a human selected (M7). Selection is an editorial judgment
  and we are not pretending otherwise.
