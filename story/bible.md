# RIYA CALLING — Story Bible

> *"Jo teen din pehle chali gayi... uska phone aa raha hai."*
> (She died three days ago. She's calling.)

**Series:** Riya Calling · 5 episodes · Hinglish suspense-thriller · Bhopal
**Format:** reconvergent branching audio drama — 4 checkpoints per playthrough, 30 unique paths, 5 endings.
**Replaces** *Aakhri Awaaz* as the canonical demo story. `files/story.md` is dead; this directory is the source of truth.

---

## 1. Logline

Three nights after his little sister Riya dies in a Diwali-night "accident", Arjun's
phone rings at 2:07 AM. Caller ID: **Riya 💜**. She's alive, laughing, teasing him —
calling from three days in the past, three days before she dies. Arjun has three
nights of phone calls to save her. But every change he makes, death re-routes —
because it was never an accident. Someone is going to kill Riya on Diwali night,
and the only person who can catch the killer is the victim herself.

**Why this premise for THIS demo:** the entire story happens through a phone.
Calls, voice notes, texts, missed calls — the listener's device isn't a gimmick
bolted onto the story; it IS the story. And "fate re-routes every change" is the
in-fiction justification for reconvergent branching: whatever the listener
chooses, the story credibly arrives at the next checkpoint, and the listener
never feels the rails — they feel *fate*.

## 2. The rules of the phone-link (canon, never violated)

1. **The offset is exactly 3 days.** Arjun's "tonight" ↔ Riya's "three nights ago."
   Both timelines advance in lockstep: one night passes for him = one night for her.
   Contact nights: Night 1 ↔ her D-3 (pre-dawn), Night 2 ↔ her D-2 night
   (Ep2+Ep3 share this window), Night 3 ↔ her D-1, and her Diwali night (the
   night she dies) lands on **his Night 4**.
2. **The window.** Night 1: the link opens only 2:07–3:33 AM. Each night it opens
   earlier and holds longer (Night 2 opens 9:07 PM and holds past midnight —
   Ep2 and Ep3 are one continuous night) — by Night 4 (Diwali) it is open
   continuously. Nobody
   explains this; the link is the one unexplained mystery in the story. Everything
   else has a rational answer.
3. **Physical changes ripple instantly.** If Riya buries a note in the garden on
   her Tuesday, it's in the garden — three days weathered — the moment she does it.
   (This is how Arjun proves it's real, and how they pass evidence across time.)
4. **The death record does NOT change in advance.** History between her "now" and
   her Diwali is *pending*. The obituary, the police file, the cremation — all hold
   until her Diwali night re-occurs at the end of his Night 3. Then history
   rewrites — **live, at 11:58 PM, while he's on the call.** (This is the climax
   mechanic of Ep5.)
5. **Every change has a price: a memory.** Each time the past meaningfully shifts,
   Arjun loses one memory of Riya. Small at first — he forgets the Nainital trip;
   Maa mentions it and he draws a blank. The price escalates. Ending E5 ("11:58")
   spends everything. Established in Ep2, paid in full in Ep5.
   *Canon exception (E5 only):* the link takes its payment from whoever asks for
   the change. Zoya, who files the one present→past change (the barricade), pays
   differently — she keeps her memory of the erased three days. She becomes the
   only person who remembers the timeline that no longer happened.
6. **Fate's inertia is not supernatural.** Changes keep failing to save her because
   the killer has a **deadline** — his anticipatory-bail hearing is the morning
   after Diwali, and Riya's evidence reaches the court that day unless she's dead
   before it. He is *hunting* her all three days. Move her, and the method changes;
   the date can't. The story lets the audience believe in fate for two episodes,
   then hands them the rational floor beneath it. The dread survives the reveal.

## 3. The crime (what actually happened — writers' truth)

Eleven months ago, Riya's best friend **Sana Qureshi** died in a hit-and-run.
Driver: **Rohan Chaddha** — drunk, son of MLA Brijmohan Chaddha. Case buried in
weeks: watchman "unsaw" it, CCTV drive "corrupted", Sana's family paid and moved.

Riya — 19, journalism student, runs a tiny true-crime college podcast called
**"Khoji"** — never accepted it. For a year she quietly collected evidence *as
audio*: a voice note of the watchman describing the car before he was bought; a
recording of Rohan's garage manager discussing the smashed headlight invoice;
Sana's last voicemail. Everything lives on **one SD card** hidden in her podcast
recorder. (All the evidence is audio. In an audio drama. On an audio platform.
This is deliberate.)

Her mistake: she told her boyfriend **Dev** she was "almost done" and about to
send everything to a crime-branch contact before the Chaddha bail hearing — the
morning after Diwali. Dev, terrified for her (and whose father's dealership
franchise lives at Chaddha's mercy), told Rohan's manager, believing they'd just
"warn her off." Instead, on Diwali night at 11:41 PM, a message lured her out — sent from
Ramdin the watchman's phone, under duress: *"Beta, woh log mere ghar pe hain.
Tune jo diya tha... maine wapas de diya. Aake le ja. Warna woh mujhe"* — it
ends mid-sentence. She went. Rohan's SUV forced her scooty off the bypass
flyover at 11:58 PM. Verdict: accident. Case
closed in two days. MLA's son was never even named.

**Riya died because she was about to win.** That's the engine of the whole story.

## 4. Cast

### ARJUN MISHRA (26) — the listener's POV
Wedding videographer, Bhopal. Father died 2019; he's been half-brother,
half-father since. On Diwali night he was editing a wedding in his studio with
his phone on silent. **Riya called him three times while she was being lured out:
11:41, 11:47, 11:53. He missed all three.** The story opens with him staring at
those missed calls. His arc: guilt → disbelief → mission. The listener makes
Arjun's choices; at checkpoints, the question is always some form of *what do
you tell her to do?*
Voice: low, tired, dry humour that cracks. Calls her *"Riyu"*. When he's scared
he goes very quiet, not loud.

### RIYA MISHRA (19) — the girl on the line
Fast-talking, fearless, funny — the room gets louder when she enters. Calls
Arjun *"Buddhu bhaiya"*, drags every sentence ("bhaiyaaaa"). Runs "Khoji" from
the hostel-room closet of her college media lab. Grief for Sana is the quiet
floor under all her noise. Crucially: **she is not a victim awaiting rescue; she
is the detective.** Arjun has hindsight; Riya has hands. He knows *that* she
dies; she finds out *why*. The listener directs her — she executes, improvises,
and talks back. (She is also the emotional AI-persona: at set moments the
listener can just *talk* to her.)
Voice: bright, quick, mock-offended; whispers when it matters and the whisper is
worse than a scream.

### ZOYA KHAN (28) — the advice agent (live AI persona)
Sub-Inspector, Cyber Cell, Bhopal — Arjun's college batch, the one who stayed
sharp. Dry, procedural, zero patience for grief-mysticism — until Arjun shows
her the buried note (three days weathered, ink one day old; lab-datable,
impossible). Her arc: skeptic → co-conspirator in the world's strangest case.
**Demo role:** the callable AI persona. The listener (as Arjun) can phone Zoya
between beats and *ask advice* — she reasons over the listener's actual state
(flags, evidence collected, checkpoint history), never spoils past
`episode_progress`, and gives read-of-the-case, not answers.
Voice: clipped Hinglish, chai-slurp pauses, "dekho Arjun—" before every hard truth.

### DEV SAXENA (21) — the betrayer who loves her
Riya's boyfriend. Sweet, weak, in over his head. His leak got her killed and he
doesn't fully know it. Track A (Ep4A) is his reckoning: confession, and — if the
listener chooses — redemption at the highest price.

### ROHAN CHADDHA (28) — the killer
MLA's son. Not a mastermind — worse: an entitled man with one night to erase a
girl. Drunk-driving killer of Sana; now methodical only because his lawyer gave
him a date. Menace register: polite in public, feral when cornered. He never
learns anything about the listener's real world (hard rule inherited from
rules.md §4).

### Supporting
**MLA Brijmohan Chaddha** — the weather, not a character; felt in every cop's
hesitation. **KALIA** — Rohan's driver/fixer; the SUV. The watchman **Ramdin** —
Sana's case's broken witness; his voice note is evidence; his lure-message is
the murder weapon's handle. **Maa (Sudha)** — sedated grief in Ep1, steel by
Ep5. **Sana Qureshi** — dead before page 1; her last voicemail is the story's
conscience.

mid-episode spike + hard cliffhanger at every episode end; the series' biggest
cliffhanger sits at the Ep3→4 boundary (Pocket FM's paywall slot) — which is
exactly where our fork (CP3) lands.

## 6. Themes & register

- **Grief bargaining, literalized.** "If I could talk to her one more time" — he can.
  Every listener who has lost someone is instantly inside the premise.
- **The missed calls.** Guilt as fuel. The phone he silenced is the phone that rings.
- **Fate vs choice** — the branching architecture IS the theme. The story keeps
  asking: can you change it? (Endings answer: yes, no, and *at what price*.)
- **Justice is audio.** A girl with a podcast recorder vs a dynasty. Her voice
  outlives her — in ending E4, it literally convicts.
- Register: conversational Hinglish, Bhopal texture (bypass flyover, Ashoka
  Garden, MP Nagar showroom, chai at 2 AM). Narration novel-style but minimal —
  the phone carries the story. Melodrama beats earned at act turns, never
  constant (per Pocket FM writer guidance: calibrate each cliffhanger to the
  protagonist's emotional state, not mechanics).

## 7. Privacy & safety rails (inherited, binding)

- No character ever references the listener's real name/location/life. Menace
  stays inside the fiction (rules.md §4).
- mic_listen remains amplitude-only. Original IP, synthetic voices, no cloning.
- Rohan's violence: felt, never gratuitous; the fatal moment is always sound
  design (dhol, SUV engine, silence), never gore.

## 8. Canon quick-facts (agents must never contradict)

- Sana died 11 months ago; hit-and-run; Rohan drove; case buried.
- Riya's evidence = ONE SD card of audio, hidden in her Khoji recorder in the
  college media lab (moves in Ep4B).
- Bail hearing = morning after Diwali. Rohan's deadline. Fate's floor.
- The lure message on Diwali night came "from the watchman Ramdin" (sent under
  duress from his phone).
- Riya's last three calls to Arjun: 11:41, 11:47, 11:53 PM. Death: 11:58 PM.
- The link costs memories: Nainital trip (Ep2), her voice in the childhood
  video (Ep3), escalating; E5 spends all memories of the three nights.
- Dev leaked to Rohan's showroom manager, not to Rohan directly; he believed
  it meant a warning, not a hit.
- Zoya knows only what Arjun shows her + anything up to listener's
  `episode_progress`. She never learns the future.
- Riya never learns the exact manner of her original death unless the listener's
  path reveals it (flag-gated; see branch-map).
